The space

A style all
of its own.

Formerly an artist's studio, this creative enclave has been converted into a wonderfully quirky weekend retreat. Nestled in the Noosa hinterland looking up at Mount Eerwah, it has an outdoor clawfoot bath fed from the creek, a shower with exposed copper pipes, vine-wrapped beams and an outdoor firepit. A one-of-a-kind nature hideaway.

The bath, and how it works

The clawfoot bath on the deck is a bit of fun. It isn't connected to the hot water system or the tank — every drop that feeds it comes from the creek, so its clarity depends entirely on the weather. You may find some discolouration and even a little river sand. Think of it as a rather unique paddling pool, best taken on a hot afternoon.

Inside

A bed under a timber ceiling with windows on three sides, so you wake up inside the canopy. A dark green kitchen that looks straight into the bush — refrigerator, microwave, espresso machine, kettle, toaster, wine glasses, a dining table and enough cookware to mean it. Books on the shelf, a TV if you want one, air conditioning for the hot months.

Outside

A private patio, outdoor furniture, an outdoor dining area, and a firepit on the lawn with Mount Eerwah rising straight behind it. A timber footbridge crosses the creek. The backyard is shared and not fully fenced, which is worth knowing if you're bringing a dog with opinions.

One honest note

The wood-burning stove out on the deck is decorative only — please don't light it. The firepit on the lawn is the real one, and it's better anyway.

Good to know

Before you come.

The bath is cold. On purpose.

Creek-fed, not plumbed. Expect river sand and a bit of colour after rain. It is the best thing here on a hot February afternoon and nobody's idea of a spa.

The deck stove is decorative

Please don't light the wood burner on the deck. The firepit on the lawn is laid and ready, with the mountain for a backdrop.

No heating, so bring a jumper

There's air conditioning for the heat and plenty of blankets, but no heater. Hinterland nights in July get genuinely cool.

Light the fire.
Watch the mountain go dark.

Two-night minimum. Check in after 2pm, out by 10am.
