The short version

You can be at a market by eight, up a mountain by ten, and in the sea by lunchtime.

Eerwah Vale sits in the middle of everything without being in the middle of anything. The hinterland is behind you, the coast is in front of you, and almost nothing worth seeing is more than forty minutes away.

Every drive time below was checked on Google Maps from Eerwah Vale, without traffic. Add a bit for a Saturday morning near the markets, and a lot for school holidays anywhere near Noosa.

8 minutes · 7 km

The Eumundi
Markets

The reason most people have heard of Eumundi. Running since 1979 and now the biggest art and craft market in the country — somewhere over 350 stalls sprawling under the fig trees, with live music and more coffee than is strictly sensible.

  • Wednesdays and Saturdays, roughly 7am to 2pm — Saturday is the big one
  • Go early. By ten the car parks are a competitive sport
  • Makers, produce, plants, hot food, and a great deal of tie-dye
  • The village itself is worth an hour afterwards — two pubs, good bakeries

8 minutes · 6.5 km

Up Mount
Eerwah

The mountain you can see from the firepit. The trail starts at the end of Mount Eerwah Road and goes almost straight up — about 3km return, roughly 300 metres of climb, and around two hours if you stop at the top for a while, which you will.

  • Steep, rocky and genuinely hands-on — there are fixed ropes on the worst pitches
  • Wear proper shoes. It gets slick after rain and the descent is the hard part
  • The view from the top runs from the Conondales all the way out to the sea
  • Stop at the summit — the second peak beyond it is private land

Not a stroll. If you want flat, walk the road down the valley instead — it's beautiful and the wallabies don't mind.

33 minutes · 30 km

Noosa & the
coastal walk

Main Beach faces north, which is why the water is flat and warm and everyone learns to surf there. But the real reason to go is the headland: the coastal track through Noosa National Park runs past Tea Tree Bay and Granite Bay out to Hell's Gates.

  • The full headland circuit is about 5km — easy underfoot, shade most of the way
  • Look up in the gums near the start; there are usually koalas
  • Alexandria Bay is the wild one, and a longer walk in
  • Hastings Street afterwards for lunch, or Noosa Junction for less of a queue

32 minutes to the launch · 41 km

The Noosa
Everglades

There are only two everglade systems on earth, and this is the other one. The upper Noosa River runs through the Cooloola section of Great Sandy National Park between walls of paperbark, over water stained dark by tea tree — so still, and so black, that it doubles everything above it. They call the best stretch the River of Mirrors.

  • Launch from Boreen Point or Elanda Point on Lake Cootharaba, half an hour from the gate
  • The Narrows, just past Kinaba, is the mirrored stretch everyone comes for
  • Paddle up to Harry's Hut — a historic hut and day-use area, good for lunch and a swim
  • Hire a kayak and go your own way, or take a guided paddle or an eco boat cruise
  • Go early or late. Wind ruins the reflections, and it usually arrives by mid-morning

39 minutes · 38 km

Booloumba Creek
& the Conondales

The best day out on this list, and the one that takes the most planning. Booloumba Creek runs through Conondale National Park in a series of granite chutes, waterfalls and deep green swimming holes — Booloumba Falls, and the Artists Cascades further up.

  • Check your car first. The access road is gravel with three creek crossings and really wants a high-clearance 4WD
  • In a 2WD, park on the roadside before the crossings and walk in — it's a kilometre or so extra each way
  • The Strangler Cairn is worth the trip on its own: an Andy Goldsworthy sculpture, 3.7m of hand-cut granite with a strangler fig growing out of the top, slowly swallowing it. About 6.5km return
  • No phone signal, no shop, no fuel. Bring lunch and more water than you think

20 minutes · 25 km

Kenilworth,
for eating

A one-street dairy town in the Mary Valley that has quietly become a food stop. The drive over Eumundi–Kenilworth Road is half the pleasure — it climbs, twists and drops you into farmland.

  • Kenilworth Country Bakery — home of the 1kg donut challenge, with walls of engraved plates for everyone who finished one. Coffee comes served inside a donut, with Nutella at the bottom
  • Kenilworth Dairies — cheese made on site, a viewing window onto the factory floor, tastings, and Poppa's Café for handmade ice cream
  • The Kenilworth Hotel for a beer on the verandah
  • Carry on to Booloumba Creek from here — it's the same road

32 min

Boreen Point

A handful of houses on the edge of Lake Cootharaba, and one very good pub with a lawn running down to the water. Where you set off for the Everglades, and worth an afternoon on its own.

27 min

Coolum & Peregian

Where locals go when Noosa is full. Patrolled surf, a low-key strip of cafés, and Mount Coolum next door if you want a short sharp climb with a view.

38 min

Montville & Maleny

The high hinterland, up on the Blackall Range. Galleries, long views west over the Glass House Mountains, and waterfalls at Kondalilla and Mapleton on the way.

8 min

Eumundi on a non-market day

Quieter, and arguably better. Two old timber pubs, a brewery, good coffee, and the whole village to yourself on a Tuesday.

Before you set off

Four things worth knowing.

Market days are Wednesday and Saturday

Every week, year round, roughly 7am to 2pm. Saturday is much bigger and much busier. If you only want the produce, go Wednesday.

Booloumba needs the right car

Three creek crossings on gravel. High-clearance 4WD, or park before the first crossing and walk. Check the park alerts before you go — it closes after heavy rain.

Mount Eerwah is a real climb

Short, but steep and rocky with rope sections. Not one for flip-flops, small children, or the morning after rain.

Fuel and groceries

Eumundi has the basics. For a proper shop, Cooroy is fifteen minutes. There is nothing at all out towards the Conondales, so fill up first.

Or stay put.
That works too.

Two studios, five acres, a firepit and a horse. Nobody has ever complained about a day spent entirely on the deck.
