The space

Contemporary,
creative, and quiet.

Pick mangoes, bush lemons and star fruit and watch wallabies roam the five acres around this organically converted former art studio. Inside, the original wood detailing, the feature tin wall and the wood-burning stove all add to the romantic ambience.

What's inside

A king bed, a spacious bathroom, and a living area under three-metre ceilings with a sofa and a dining table. The conversion was done slowly and kept what was worth keeping — the original timber, and the sheet of old tin behind the chimney of the wood stove.

The galley kitchen is well stocked with cutlery, pots, pans and cooking utensils. There's a microwave, a bar fridge, a two-ring electric hob, a kettle, a toaster and a mini bake-and-grill oven. Enough for a very good breakfast and a decent dinner; not enough for a dinner party, which is rather the point.

Two decks, two times of day

French doors open onto an open deck with a table and chairs — for eating outside, or a glass of wine at sunset. On the north side there's another covered deck with two Adirondack chairs facing the mango tree: the place to take your first cup of tea, with the birds and the wallabies going about their morning. In the evening, it's a lovely place to light the fire under the stars.

The art bit, if you want it

This was our studio. The easels and art supplies are still here and you're welcome to use them — small canvases, papers, paints, charcoal and artists' pencils. Plenty of guests never touch them. Some leave things behind.

Getting in

Open the gate outwards, leaving a car space in front of you, and shut it behind you — we have a tame horse, Shanika, who roams the property. Drive up past the main house right to the top (give it a bit at the steep part just before the parking area) and the Art Shed is there with ample parking outside. You'll have your own key and private access.
